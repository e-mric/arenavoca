The later movement is a simple production placeholder, not a simulated full football engine. The original passing source is also consumed by the content preparation script, keeping the match and diagram in agreement.

## Reproduce

From `app/`:

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup --python scripts/render-match.py
node scripts/prepare-content.mjs
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i content-source/frames/frame-%04d.png -i content-source/commentary-en.mp3 -filter_complex '[1:a]adelay=6000|6000,apad[a]' -map 0:v -map '[a]' -c:v libx264 -preset fast -crf 24 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 96k -t 30 -movflags +faststart public/media/match.mp4
```

Blender 5.2.2 LTS was used for authoring, with Workbench rendering at 1280×720, 30 fps. The final clip is exactly 30 s and 512,507 bytes. The generated frame sequence is excluded from Git; the editable `.blend`, script, movement data, and final MP4 are retained.
