# Learning Spanish, and learning to build

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

## The learning loop

Watch an action → notice a short Spanish phrase → optionally reveal meaning or hear it → recall it later → use it in a small story or quiz. The match continues while the learner chooses their level of attention. Native-language support can ease the start; do not imply that the current recording has automatic alternate commentary or dubbing.

The [research review](../SPANISH_THROUGH_SPORT_RESEARCH.md) connects this design to captioned viewing, retrieval practice, spacing, and cognitive-load research. Those studies motivate design choices; they do not prove that ArenaVoca improves retention. A learner pilot and fluent Spanish/audio review remain necessary.

## Keep three kinds of content distinct

| Content | What makes it trustworthy |
| --- | --- |
| Actual match moment | Recording timestamp and traceable evidence; distinguish raw AI output from reviewed content. |
| Historical sports story | Sources for factual claims and clear treatment of uncertainty. |
| Fictional practice story | Explicit fiction label; suitable language and a coherent sports situation. |

The mobile prepared match pack and the newer Match Lab pack are separate artifacts. Their card/story totals need not match. Check each pack's current source rather than describing them as one universally synchronized collection. See [README](../../README.md) and [PRD](../../PRD.md).

## The five-minute demo angle

“I learned VR by building a place to learn Spanish through sport.” The useful progression is an initial idea, the first headset mismatch, a spatial interaction, a learning moment, and the engineering needed to make it dependable. Use the [demo story](../DEMO_STORY.md) for the timed English script and [deck notes](../demo/README.md) for presentation assets.

The Way of Code and the Rick Rubin meme provide a creative framing: taste, curiosity, and direction help explore an experience. Engineering adds explicit constraints, small changes, tests, source review, and real-device feedback. This is our project interpretation, not a technical method attributed to Rubin. The meme's adapted coding caption must not be presented as a verified verbatim interview quote.

The [AI collaboration guide](../AI_COLLABORATION.md) turns this into a working habit: provide context, choose one behavior, verify it, then explain the tradeoff. A good learning check is whether the developer can describe the input, rule, output, failure case, and evidence without reading the generated code aloud.

## Open checks before claiming success

- Do learners remember and use a phrase after the session, rather than only recognize it immediately?
- Does spatial interaction help the activity enough to justify wearing the headset?
- Can the demo distinguish working features, reviewed examples, and future plans in five minutes?
