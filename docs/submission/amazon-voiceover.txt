What if watching football could help you practise Spanish, one moment at a time?

This is ArenaVoca, running on Amazon's Vega simulator. Watching comes first. Learning is there when you want it.

Our demonstration follows Barcelona against Madrid, from October twenty-eighth, twenty eighteen. The match footage comes from FC Barcelona's official YouTube channel.

Jordi Alba runs into space, passes the ball back, and Coutinho scores the opening goal. Three connected actions become three small opportunities to learn.

As the action unfolds, matching moments become available. Nothing opens automatically. With the remote, I choose a moment when I am ready.

Here is the pass back. The illustrated card connects the movement to its Spanish phrase: Alba pasa el balón hacia atrás.

I can reveal the English underneath: Alba passes the ball back. The match keeps playing, while my selected card stays in place.

I save the phrase and return to full-screen watching. There is no exercise to finish before I can enjoy the game.

Later, Saved brings me back to the phrases I chose. Practise asks me to recall the meaning. If I get it wrong, feedback helps me try again. My saved cards stay on this device.

The TV app uses React Native with Amazon's Kepler framework and media player. The video's position determines which prepared moments are available. Opening a card does not pause or seek the match.

The illustrations and learning content are prepared ahead of time. This prototype does not claim live match recognition or phone synchronisation.

ArenaVoca. Watch. Notice. Remember.
